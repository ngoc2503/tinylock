Hiccups are very common in newborns and young children. Many parents worry when their baby repeatedly makes a "hic" sound, especially after feeding, eating, or while sleeping. However, hiccups are usually a normal physiological phenomenon, harmless, and temporary.

To understand why children get hiccups so often, parents should learn how hiccups occur, what factors may trigger them, and how to respond safely.

## 1. What are hiccups?

Hiccups occur when the diaphragm, a muscle between the chest and abdomen that plays an important role in breathing, suddenly contracts involuntarily.

When the diaphragm contracts unexpectedly, air is drawn in rapidly. The vocal cords then close suddenly, producing the characteristic "hic" sound.

Hiccups usually last for a short time and stop on their own. Newborns and young infants experience hiccups more frequently than adults, particularly during the first months of life. In most cases, hiccups do not cause pain or significantly affect a child's health.

## 2. Why do children get hiccups so often?

Several factors may stimulate the diaphragm and trigger hiccups in children.

### Eating too quickly or too much

When a child feeds too quickly, continuously, or consumes too much in one feeding, the stomach may become distended. This stretching can stimulate the diaphragm and trigger hiccups.

This is a common cause in newborns and young children, especially when bottle-feeding or when a baby is very hungry and feeds rapidly.

### Swallowing too much air during feeding

Children may swallow extra air while feeding, especially when:

- They cry excessively before feeding.
- They feed too quickly.
- Their feeding position is not appropriate.
- The bottle nipple has a flow that is too fast.
- They do not latch effectively during breastfeeding.

Air trapped in the stomach may cause abdominal distension and increase the likelihood of hiccups.

### Stomach distension or irritation

After a large feeding, the stomach expands and may stimulate the diaphragm. This is why some children develop hiccups immediately after feeding or eating.

In some children, hiccups may occur alongside spit-up or reflux symptoms. However, hiccups alone do not mean that a child has gastroesophageal reflux disease.

### Emotional changes

In older children, laughing excessively, excitement, anxiety, or nervousness may alter breathing patterns and stimulate the reflex that causes hiccups.

A child may develop hiccups while playing, running, laughing, or after an emotionally stimulating situation.

### Other possible triggers

Hiccups sometimes occur without an identifiable cause. Certain factors, such as spicy foods, carbonated drinks, changes in beverage temperature, or irritation of the throat and stomach, may also trigger hiccups in older children.

Most ordinary hiccups are not associated with a serious medical condition.

## 3. Are hiccups dangerous for children?

Hiccups are usually harmless and do not require treatment. Children can continue feeding, eating, sleeping, and playing normally even when they occasionally have hiccups.

Parents generally do not need to worry if their child:

- Has hiccups for a few minutes and then stops spontaneously.
- Develops hiccups after feeding or eating without discomfort.
- Continues feeding well, eating normally, and growing appropriately.
- Does not have unusual symptoms such as difficulty breathing, repeated vomiting, or abdominal pain.

However, if hiccups last a long time, occur frequently enough to interfere with feeding, eating, or sleeping, or are accompanied by other symptoms, the child should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

## 4. What should you do when your child has hiccups?

### For newborns and milk-fed babies

If your baby starts hiccupping during a feeding, parents can:

- Pause the feeding and keep the baby comfortable.
- Hold the baby upright while properly supporting the head and neck.
- Try gentle burping.
- If the baby is crying or extremely hungry, help them calm down before continuing the feeding.
- Resume feeding when the hiccups have eased or the baby is comfortable.

For bottle-fed babies, pay attention to the nipple flow rate and avoid allowing the baby to feed too quickly. For breastfed babies, observe the feeding position and latch.

Not every baby needs to burp after every feeding. If the baby is comfortable, there is no need to burp them for an extended period.

### For older children

If an older child has hiccups, parents can encourage them to:

- Sit down and relax.
- Take small sips of water if they are old enough to drink water safely.
- Breathe slowly and gently.
- Avoid eating more when they are already full.
- Wait for the hiccups to stop before resuming vigorous activity.

Most cases resolve spontaneously without any special intervention.

### What should parents avoid?

Parents should avoid using potentially dangerous folk remedies, including:

- Frightening the child to make the hiccups stop.
- Forcing the child to hold their breath for a long time.
- Blocking the child's nose or mouth.
- Giving lemon juice, vinegar, or spicy substances to stop hiccups.
- Giving water, sugar, or herbal drinks to newborns without medical advice.
- Giving medication for hiccups without a doctor's recommendation.

Never try to make the child vomit, press firmly on the abdomen, or perform maneuvers that involve manipulating the neck to stop hiccups.

## 5. How can you reduce the frequency of hiccups?

Hiccups cannot always be prevented. However, parents can reduce some common triggers by adjusting their child's feeding and daily habits.

### For newborns and infants

- Feed the baby when early hunger cues appear, rather than waiting until they become extremely hungry and cry excessively.
- Feed the baby in a comfortable position with appropriate head and neck support.
- If the baby feeds too quickly, pause to allow them to rest.
- For bottle-fed babies, check the nipple to ensure the milk does not flow too quickly.
- Try burping the baby during or after feeding if they appear to swallow a lot of air.
- If hiccups frequently occur during feeding, try adjusting the feeding position and timing.

### For older children

- Encourage them to eat slowly and chew thoroughly.
- Avoid eating too much at one time.
- Limit carbonated drinks if these tend to trigger hiccups.
- Avoid running or laughing excessively while eating.
- Make mealtimes relaxed and avoid pressuring children to eat quickly.

These measures may reduce some triggers, but they cannot guarantee that hiccups will never occur.

## 6. When should you take your child to a doctor?

Parents should seek medical advice if:

- Hiccups continue for more than 48 hours.
- Hiccups occur frequently and interfere with feeding, eating, or sleeping.
- The child has hiccups accompanied by repeated vomiting or abdominal pain.
- The child feeds poorly, refuses food, or loses weight.
- The child appears unusually uncomfortable, cries persistently, or becomes excessively tired.
- Hiccups occur alongside other symptoms that concern the parents.

Persistent hiccups are rarely associated with digestive, neurological, or respiratory problems, or medication side effects. A doctor will assess the child's condition to determine whether further investigation is necessary.

## 7. Warning signs that require emergency medical care

Hiccups alone are generally not an emergency. However, seek emergency care immediately if your child develops:

- Difficulty breathing, abnormal breathing, or bluish skin.
- Signs of choking, including an inability to cough, cry, or breathe normally.
- Reduced responsiveness, unusual drowsiness, or difficulty waking.
- Repeated vomiting, inability to feed or drink, and signs of dehydration.
- Severe pain or rapidly worsening health.

If you suspect choking, follow age-appropriate choking first-aid guidance and call your local emergency number when necessary.

## 8. Frequently asked questions

### Is it normal for newborns to hiccup several times a day?

Yes, it can be normal. Newborns often hiccup several times a day, especially after feeding. If the baby feeds well, sleeps normally, breathes comfortably, and has no other concerning symptoms, parents generally do not need to intervene.

### Should I wake my baby if they hiccup during sleep?

There is no need to wake a baby solely because of hiccups if they are sleeping comfortably, breathing normally, and showing no unusual symptoms. Continue following safe sleep recommendations: place the baby on their back on a firm, flat surface without pillows or inclined sleep devices.

### Are hiccups a sign of acid reflux?

Not necessarily. Hiccups may occur in babies with reflux, but hiccups alone are not enough to diagnose the condition. If your baby frequently spits up, vomits, appears uncomfortable during feeding, feeds poorly, or does not gain weight appropriately, seek medical advice.

### Do hiccups affect a child's development?

Ordinary hiccups that occur for a short time do not affect a child's development. Only prolonged hiccups or those associated with an underlying condition that interferes with feeding or sleep may require medical evaluation and treatment.

## Conclusion

Children frequently experience hiccups because of sudden, involuntary contractions of the diaphragm. Feeding too quickly, swallowing excess air, stomach distension, or emotional changes may trigger them. Hiccups are usually a normal physiological phenomenon, particularly in newborns.

Parents should remain calm, adjust feeding habits, help the child rest, and allow the hiccups to resolve naturally. Avoid dangerous remedies or medications without medical advice. If hiccups persist, interfere with daily activities, or occur alongside unusual symptoms, seek medical evaluation.
