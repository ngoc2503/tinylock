Discipline does not mean punishment. Its goal is to help children learn appropriate behavior while still feeling safe and loved.

## How discipline differs from punishment

- **Discipline** focuses on teaching what to do and why.
- **Punishment** focuses on making a child afraid or hurt, so children often learn only how to avoid getting caught.
- Discipline targets the **behavior**, not the **child** ("Hitting is not okay", rather than "You are a bad kid").

## Key principles

1. **Praise good behavior.** Children need to know when they get it right. Be specific: "You put your toys away by yourself. That was very responsible."
2. **Be a role model.** Children learn more from watching you than from listening to you.
3. **Set clear, short and consistent limits.** Rules that keep changing confuse children.
4. **Use reasonable consequences linked to the behavior.** For example, if a toy is thrown, it is put away for a while.
5. **Listen to your child's feelings.** They are allowed to feel angry, but not to hit.
6. **Redirect young children.** For little ones, moving to another activity often works better than repeating "no".
7. **Learn from mistakes, including your own.** If you lose your temper, calm down and apologize. That shows your child how to repair a mistake.

## Why to avoid harsh punishment

Current parenting guidance suggests that spanking and harsh yelling do not lead to better behavior in the long run and may increase behavior problems. Teaching with patience and consistency usually works better.

## Ideas by age

- **Under 3 years:** model the behavior, make the environment safe, redirect, keep routines steady.
- **Preschool (3-6 years):** set a few simple rules, praise what they do right, offer small choices.
- **School age and older:** discuss the rules together, explain the reasons, let your child help decide consequences.

## When to seek professional help

If your child's behavior is very hard to manage, lasts a long time, or leaves you exhausted, talk to your pediatrician or a child psychologist. Counseling or parent training can be very helpful.
