Enough regular sleep supports a child's growth, mood and behavior during the day. Sleep needs change with age, and every child differs a little.

## How much sleep children need

These are common reference ranges over 24 hours, naps included:

- **Babies 4-12 months:** about 12-16 hours
- **Toddlers 1-2 years:** about 11-14 hours
- **Preschoolers 3-5 years:** about 10-13 hours

If your child sleeps a little less but is cheerful and plays and eats normally during the day, it is usually fine.

## Build a bedtime routine

1. **Keep a fixed bedtime and wake-up time**, even on weekends (within about an hour).
2. **Repeat a short sequence** each night: bath, pajamas, a story, lights out.
3. **Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool.**
4. **Avoid screens** (TV, phone, tablet) for at least an hour before bed.
5. **Avoid sugary snacks and very active play** in the evening.

## When your child will not sleep

- Stay calm and keep it short and gentle: "It's bedtime now."
- Walk your child back to bed each time they get up, without long arguments.
- Offer a comfort item (a soft toy or small blanket) if it suits their age.
- For young children learning to fall asleep alone, you can stay nearby and gradually step back over several nights.

> Changing sleep habits takes days to weeks. Being patient and consistent matters more than being fast.

## Safe sleep for babies

- Place your baby on their back on a firm, flat surface.
- Keep soft bedding, pillows and toys out of the crib.
- Ask your pediatrician for the latest safe sleep guidance for your baby's age.

## When to talk to a doctor

Talk to your pediatrician if:

- Your child snores loudly, wheezes or seems to stop breathing during sleep
- Sleep problems last for weeks, or your child wakes many times each night
- Your child is very sleepy during the day, or is often irritable or unable to focus
- You are worried about your child's sleep or feel completely worn out
