When a baby suddenly coughs, chokes, turns red, or has milk coming out of their mouth while bottle-feeding, it can be frightening for parents. Some babies cough because milk flows too quickly, while others may have difficulty coordinating sucking, swallowing, and breathing.

So, what should you do if your baby frequently chokes while bottle-feeding? Parents need to know how to respond, adjust feeding positions, choose an appropriate bottle nipple, and recognize signs that require medical attention.

## 1. Signs that your baby may be choking during bottle-feeding

Your baby may show one or more of the following signs:

- Sudden coughing or choking while feeding.
- Milk leaking from the corners of the mouth or coming out through the nose.
- Repeatedly pulling away from the bottle, becoming uncomfortable, or crying.
- Frequent swallowing, loud swallowing sounds, or visible difficulty swallowing.
- Wheezing, wet-sounding breathing, or gurgling sounds during or after feeding.
- Changes in skin color, unusual paleness, or blue lips during feeding.

Not all these symptoms mean that your baby's airway is blocked. However, if they happen repeatedly, especially with changes in breathing or skin color, your baby should be medically assessed.

## 2. Why does a baby choke while bottle-feeding?

Several factors can cause a baby to cough or choke during bottle-feeding.

### Milk is flowing too quickly

This is a common cause. If the bottle nipple has a flow rate that is too fast or the bottle is held too vertically, milk may enter your baby's mouth faster than they can comfortably suck and swallow.

Your baby may cough, choke, pull away from the nipple, or have milk leaking from the mouth.

### The bottle nipple is not suitable

Bottle nipples have different flow rates. If the nipple flows too quickly for your baby's feeding abilities, they may need to swallow too rapidly and become more likely to choke.

Never cut or enlarge the nipple opening yourself to increase milk flow.

### Incorrect feeding position

If your baby is lying too flat, their head is tilted backward, or their neck is bent, coordinating sucking and swallowing may become more difficult.

Feeding a baby while they are lying completely on their back or propping up the bottle so they can feed alone can also increase choking risks.

### Your baby is feeding too quickly or needs more breaks

Some babies suck continuously without taking enough breaks. When too much milk enters their mouth, they may struggle to coordinate swallowing and breathing.

### Your baby has difficulty coordinating sucking, swallowing, and breathing

Newborns, particularly premature babies or those with certain neurological, muscular, or structural conditions involving the mouth and throat, may have difficulty coordinating these functions.

If your baby frequently coughs or chokes, has prolonged feeding sessions, breathes abnormally, or gains weight poorly, they need medical assessment rather than simply switching bottles.

### Other health conditions

Gastroesophageal reflux, airway abnormalities, or swallowing difficulties may also contribute to coughing and choking during feeding.

The exact cause cannot be determined from one or two choking episodes alone.

## 3. What should you do if your baby chokes during bottle-feeding?

Parents need to distinguish between two situations: a baby who can still cough and breathe, and a baby with severe airway obstruction.

### Situation 1: Your baby can cough, breathe, and cry

If your baby is coughing audibly, can still breathe, and is not turning blue:

1. Stop feeding immediately and gently remove the bottle from your baby's mouth.
2. Support your baby's head and neck, keeping the upper body slightly upright.
3. Allow your baby to cough if the cough is effective.
4. Watch their breathing, lip color, and overall condition.
5. Resume feeding only when your baby is completely settled, breathing normally, and no longer coughing or choking.

Do not pour more milk or water into your baby's mouth to help them swallow. Do not rush to resume feeding while your baby is still coughing or breathing abnormally.

If your baby repeatedly coughs or chokes, has difficulty breathing, wheezes, or shows unusual symptoms after feeding, seek medical assessment.

### Situation 2: Your baby cannot cough, cry, or breathe effectively

This may indicate severe airway obstruction.

Warning signs include:

- Your baby cannot make a sound or cry.
- They cannot cough effectively or breathe.
- Their lips, tongue, or skin turn blue.
- They become limp or unresponsive.

**Call your local emergency number immediately and begin infant choking first aid if you are able to perform it.**

Basic first-aid steps:

**Step 1: Give up to 5 back blows**

- Place your baby face-down along your forearm, with their head lower than their body.
- Support the head and jaw securely without pressing on the neck.
- Use the heel of your other hand to give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades.
- After each blow, check whether the obstruction has cleared.

**Step 2: Give up to 5 chest thrusts if the obstruction remains**

- Carefully turn your baby face-up while keeping their head lower than their body.
- Place two fingers in the center of the chest, just below the line of the nipples.
- Give up to 5 firm chest thrusts to help dislodge the obstruction.

**Step 3: Continue alternating**

If your baby is still unable to breathe, continue alternating 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts until the airway is cleared or emergency responders arrive.

If your baby becomes unresponsive, place them on a firm, flat surface and begin infant CPR according to established infant first-aid guidance. Ask someone to call emergency services if this has not already been done.

**Never do the following:**

- Do not perform the Heimlich maneuver or abdominal thrusts on a baby under one year old.
- Do not put your fingers into your baby's mouth or throat unless you can clearly see an object that can be safely removed.
- Do not hold your baby upside down by their feet.
- Do not shake or jolt your baby to make milk come out.

Important: These steps are for severe airway obstruction. They are not the routine response to a baby who briefly coughs during feeding. Parents are encouraged to take an infant first-aid course to learn the correct techniques.

## 4. How to bottle-feed your baby safely to reduce choking

If your baby frequently chokes during bottle-feeding, consider adjusting the following:

### Step 1: Choose an appropriate bottle nipple

- Choose a nipple suitable for your baby's age and feeding ability.
- If your baby frequently coughs, swallows rapidly, has milk leaking from their mouth, or repeatedly pulls away, check the nipple flow rate.
- You may discuss a slower-flow nipple with your doctor or an infant-feeding specialist.
- Never cut, enlarge, or modify the nipple opening yourself.

Do not rely only on the age label on the packaging when choosing a nipple. Every baby has different feeding abilities.

### Step 2: Adjust your baby's feeding position

- Hold your baby with their head and neck properly supported.
- Keep their head comfortably higher than their body, avoiding backward head tilting or a bent neck.
- Your baby can be held in a slightly upright position in your arms.
- Do not feed your baby while they are lying completely flat on their back.

The important point is to keep your baby's head, neck, and body stable so they can coordinate sucking, swallowing, and breathing.

### Step 3: Hold the bottle at an appropriate angle

- Gently place the nipple in your baby's mouth when they open their mouth.
- Keep the bottle nearly horizontal, tilted only enough to keep milk in the nipple.
- Avoid holding the bottle vertically, which may cause milk to flow continuously into your baby's mouth.
- Watch your baby's sucking and swallowing rhythm and adjust as needed.

This paced feeding approach helps your baby have more control over how much milk they take in.

### Step 4: Allow breaks during feeding

- Watch for signs that your baby needs a break, such as stopping sucking, releasing the nipple, or turning their head away.
- Gently remove the bottle and allow your baby to rest.
- Do not force continuous feeding or insist that your baby finish the bottle.
- You may burp your baby during or after feeding if needed.

If your baby repeatedly needs to stop because of coughing, choking, or breathing difficulties, seek medical assessment.

### Step 5: Never leave your baby to feed from a propped-up bottle

- Always have an adult present to hold and supervise your baby during feeding.
- Do not prop up the bottle with pillows, towels, or other objects.
- Never leave the bottle in your baby's mouth when you walk away.
- Do not feed your baby while you are asleep or unable to supervise them.

A propped-up bottle may allow milk to continue flowing into your baby's mouth when they cannot control the feeding pace, increasing the risk of choking.

## 5. When should you take your baby to a doctor?

Arrange a medical assessment if your baby has any of the following:

- Frequent coughing or choking during multiple feeds.
- Repeatedly pulling away from the nipple, swallowing rapidly, or having milk leak from the mouth.
- Wheezing, wet-sounding breathing, or gurgling during or after feeding.
- Frequent congestion, coughing, or discomfort while feeding.
- Unusually long feeding sessions, easy fatigue, or frequent refusal to feed.
- Poor weight gain or a noticeable decrease in milk intake.
- A previous episode of turning blue or having difficulty breathing during feeding, even if your baby later recovered.

A healthcare professional may assess your baby's feeding position, ability to latch onto the nipple, and coordination of sucking, swallowing, and breathing. If necessary, your baby may be referred to a swallowing specialist or undergo further testing.

Do not thicken milk, add cereal or powder to the bottle, or change the formula to address choking unless specifically advised by a healthcare professional.

## 6. Emergency warning signs

Seek emergency medical care immediately if your baby has:

- Difficulty breathing, pauses in breathing, or stops breathing.
- Blue lips, a blue tongue, or unusual paleness.
- Inability to cough, cry, or make sounds.
- Limpness, unresponsiveness, or difficulty waking.
- Persistent choking accompanied by breathing difficulties or rapidly worsening symptoms.

Even if your baby recovers after a severe airway obstruction episode, they should still be assessed by a healthcare professional.

## 7. Frequently asked questions

**Is it concerning if my baby chokes once or twice while bottle-feeding?**

An occasional cough may happen if milk flows too quickly or your baby has difficulty coordinating sucking and swallowing. However, if it happens repeatedly during multiple feeds, your baby should be assessed to identify the cause.

**Should I switch to a slower-flow nipple?**

If your baby shows signs of milk flowing too quickly, such as coughing, rapid swallowing, or milk leaking from the mouth, a slower-flow nipple may be worth considering. However, it should match your baby's feeding ability. Ask a healthcare professional if the problem happens frequently.

**Does choking during bottle-feeding mean my baby has reflux?**

Not necessarily. Choking may be related to milk flow, feeding position, difficulty coordinating swallowing and breathing, or other health conditions. Reflux may be one contributing factor, but it is not the only possible cause.

**Is it dangerous if milk comes out through my baby's nose during feeding?**

Milk coming out through the nose can happen when a baby coughs, spits up, or has difficulty coordinating swallowing. Stop feeding, keep your baby comfortable, and monitor their breathing. If your baby has difficulty breathing, turns blue, or this happens repeatedly, seek medical assessment.

**Can I continue feeding immediately after my baby chokes?**

No. Stop feeding and wait until your baby is calm, breathing normally, and no longer coughing or choking. If unusual symptoms persist, do not resume feeding and seek medical attention.

## Conclusion

Frequent choking during bottle-feeding may be caused by milk flowing too quickly, an unsuitable bottle nipple, an incorrect feeding position, or difficulty coordinating sucking, swallowing, and breathing. Parents should support their baby's position, adjust how they hold the bottle, allow breaks, and never prop up a bottle for independent feeding.

If your baby frequently coughs or chokes, breathes abnormally, or gains weight poorly, seek medical assessment. If your baby cannot breathe, cough, or cry effectively, provide first aid and call emergency services immediately.
