Screens can offer something useful, but for young children how they are used matters as much as how long. Suitable content, watched with an adult and not replacing play, sleep and conversation, is less likely to cause harm.

## Suggestions by age

These are common recommendations from pediatric organizations:

- **Under 18 months:** avoid screens, except video calls with family.
- **18-24 months:** if you introduce screens, choose high-quality content and watch **together**.
- **2-5 years:** limit to about 1 hour a day, favor quality content, and stay involved.
- **6 years and older:** set clear limits so screens do not crowd out sleep, physical activity, learning and family time.

## Healthier screen habits

1. **Watch with your child** and talk about it: "What do you think the character is doing?"
2. **Choose age-appropriate content** with few ads, a slower pace, and a story or learning value.
3. **Create screen-free zones and times:** the dinner table, bedrooms, the hour before bed.
4. **Avoid using screens to calm your child** often, because they will find it harder to learn to calm themselves.
5. **Set an example:** children copy how adults use phones and TV.

## Make a family media plan

- Agree together on when screens can be used and when they go off.
- Decide which devices are allowed where in the home.
- Plan alternatives in advance: reading, drawing, outdoor play, cooking together.
- Review the plan as your child grows.

## When your child cries for more screen time

- Give a warning ("Five more minutes, then we turn it off") so your child can get ready.
- Hold the rule even if they cry, and acknowledge their feelings ("I know you still want to watch").
- Offer an appealing next activity right after you switch off.

## When to talk to a doctor

Talk to your pediatrician or a child psychologist if:

- Your child has great difficulty leaving screens or has intense outbursts when they stop
- Your child sleeps poorly or loses interest in play or friends because of screens
- Your child is slow to talk or rarely interacts, and spends a lot of time on screens
- You are worried about your child's screen habits
