Frequent night waking is common during infancy. It can leave parents feeling exhausted, especially when a baby wakes repeatedly for feeding, needs comfort, or has difficulty settling back to sleep.

However, **not every baby can sleep through the night at the same age**. Sleep patterns depend on development, nutritional needs, health, and daily routines.

Rather than trying to prevent every night waking, focus on building healthy sleep habits, creating a safe sleep environment, and gradually helping your child learn to settle back to sleep when developmentally ready.

## 1. When can babies sleep through the night?

The ability to sleep for longer stretches varies by age and from one child to another.

### Babies under 3 months

Newborns have not yet developed a stable day-and-night rhythm. They usually sleep in short periods and wake frequently for feeding and care.

At this stage, parents should not expect a baby to sleep continuously throughout the night or deliberately extend the time between feeds when the baby needs to eat.

### Babies aged 3–6 months

Some babies begin sleeping for longer stretches as their circadian rhythms develop. However, many still wake for feeding or comfort.

Parents can begin establishing a consistent bedtime routine, helping the baby distinguish day from night, and watching for signs of tiredness.

### Babies aged 6–12 months

Some babies can sleep for longer periods at night. Nevertheless, they may still wake because of hunger, teething discomfort, illness, separation anxiety, or changes in their routine.

Not every waking can be prevented, and waking during the night does not automatically mean a baby has a sleep problem.

### Children over 1 year

Children often develop more predictable sleep schedules as they grow. However, developmental changes, daytime naps, their sleep environment, and emotional needs can still affect nighttime sleep.

**The important point:** “Sleeping through the night” can mean different things, including sleeping for a long stretch without needing parental help. Consider your child's age, health, and individual needs rather than comparing them with other children.

## 2. Seven ways to help your child sleep better at night

### 2.1. Keep bedtime and wake-up times reasonably consistent

Try to maintain a predictable schedule for waking up, daytime naps, and bedtime that suits your child's age.

Consistent routines help children develop a more regular sleep rhythm. However, the schedule does not need to be rigid. Adjust it according to your child's tiredness cues and individual needs.

### 2.2. Create a calming bedtime routine

Establish a short sequence of relaxing activities before bed, such as:

1. Bathing or age-appropriate personal care.
2. Changing the diaper and putting on sleepwear.
3. Dimming the lights and reducing noise.
4. Reading a book or singing a lullaby.
5. Cuddling, comforting, and settling the child for sleep.

Keep the routine simple and repeat it in roughly the same order each evening. Older children can participate in activities such as choosing a bedtime story.

### 2.3. Help your baby distinguish day from night

During the day, expose your baby to natural light and age-appropriate activities.

At night, keep lights dim, speak quietly, and minimize stimulation during feeds and diaper changes. This can gradually help your baby recognize the difference between active daytime hours and nighttime rest.

### 2.4. Watch for signs of tiredness

Common signs that a baby may be tired include:

- Yawning or rubbing their eyes.
- Becoming less interested in toys or activities.
- Becoming less active.
- Becoming irritable or having difficulty concentrating.

When you notice these signs, begin preparing your child for sleep. Waiting until a baby is overtired may make settling more difficult.

### 2.5. Make sure your baby is feeding adequately

Hunger can cause night waking, particularly during the first months of life. Feed your baby according to their age-appropriate needs and healthcare guidance.

Do not skip nighttime feeds or deliberately extend the time between feeds solely to make your baby sleep longer. If you want to reduce night feeds, consult your healthcare professional, especially if your baby is young, was born prematurely, is not gaining weight well, or has a medical condition.

### 2.6. Create a suitable sleep environment

Keep the sleep area quiet, comfortably cool, and dimly lit. Minimize sudden noise and stimulating activities before bedtime.

For babies, safe sleep is more important than trying to create an environment that makes them sleep for longer periods. Keep pillows, loose blankets, stuffed toys, and other soft objects out of an infant's sleep space.

### 2.7. Gradually help your child learn to settle back to sleep

As your child grows and becomes developmentally ready, you can support their ability to settle themselves in ways that fit your family's needs.

For example, maintain a consistent bedtime routine, put your child down when sleepy, and respond calmly when they wake. Some families choose a sleep-training approach; the decision should take into account the child's age, health, needs, and the family's preferences.

No single approach works for every child. Young infants still need responsive care, including feeding, comfort, and attention.

## 3. Safe sleep: Essential rules for parents

For infants, follow these safe-sleep practices:

- **Always place your baby on their back at the beginning of every sleep**, including naps.
- Use a firm, flat sleep surface designed for infants.
- Keep the sleep area free of pillows, loose blankets, stuffed toys, padded bumpers, and other soft objects.
- Do not routinely let a baby sleep on a sofa, armchair, or surface that is not designed for infant sleep.
- Consider room-sharing with your baby while keeping them on a separate sleep surface, especially during at least the first 6 months.
- Avoid overheating your baby. Do not put a hat on your baby indoors during sleep unless medically advised.
- Avoid bed-sharing with an infant because of the risk of suffocation and other sleep-related accidents.

If your baby begins showing signs of rolling, continue placing them on their back at the start of sleep. Stop swaddling when your baby shows signs of attempting to roll.

## 4. Common mistakes when trying to help a baby sleep through the night

### Keeping your baby awake longer during the day

Some parents assume that keeping a baby awake longer will lead to better nighttime sleep. However, overtired babies may become irritable and have more difficulty settling.

Make sure your baby gets age-appropriate daytime sleep instead of deliberately cutting naps.

### Skipping feeds when your baby still needs them

Not every baby is ready to sleep through the night without feeding. Feeding needs depend on age, development, and health.

### Frequently changing the bedtime routine

A completely different routine every evening can make it harder for a child to develop predictable sleep habits. Choose a simple routine and maintain it reasonably consistently.

### Comparing your baby's sleep with other babies

Every child develops at their own pace. Waking more often than another child of the same age does not necessarily mean you are doing something wrong.

### Using sleep products or medication without medical advice

Do not give your baby medication, supplements, or sleep aids—including melatonin—without consulting a healthcare professional.

## 5. Why might a baby wake frequently at night?

Before changing your child's sleep routine, consider possible reasons for waking:

- **Hunger:** Especially common in newborns and young babies who still need nighttime feeds.
- **A wet diaper or discomfort:** Physical discomfort may interrupt sleep.
- **Being too hot or too cold:** Inappropriate clothing or room temperature can disturb sleep.
- **Developmental changes:** Learning new skills or experiencing emotional changes can affect sleep patterns.
- **Separation anxiety:** This can occur during certain developmental stages.
- **Illness or physical discomfort:** Nasal congestion, fever, pain, or other health problems may cause night waking.

If your child's sleep changes suddenly, consider whether they have symptoms of illness or whether their routine has recently changed.

## 6. When should you seek medical advice?

Talk with your child's healthcare professional if sleep difficulties are accompanied by signs such as:

- Frequent, loud snoring, difficult breathing, or pauses in breathing during sleep.
- Breathing difficulties, bluish skin, or unusual breathing patterns.
- Persistent discomfort, pain, or unusual crying.
- Feeding difficulties, poor feeding, or inadequate weight gain.
- Frequent waking accompanied by signs of illness or a significant effect on your child's daily functioning.

If your child has difficulty breathing, turns blue, or shows other signs of a medical emergency, call emergency services immediately.

## 7. Frequently asked questions

### At what age can babies sleep through the night?

There is no single age that applies to every baby. Some begin sleeping for longer stretches during the first months, while others continue waking for feeds or comfort. Consider your baby's age, development, and nutritional needs.

### Should I wake my baby for nighttime feeds?

This depends on your baby's age, weight gain, and health. Some newborns need to be woken for feeds according to healthcare guidance, particularly if they have not regained their birth weight or are not gaining weight adequately. Ask your healthcare professional if you are unsure whether your baby can safely go longer between feeds.

### Should I let my baby cry until they fall asleep?

No single sleep-training method is right for every family. Before choosing an approach, consider your child's age, health, and care needs. Young infants need responsive care when they are hungry, uncomfortable, or in need of attention.

### Will a later bedtime help my baby sleep longer?

Not necessarily. A bedtime that is too late or becoming overtired may make it harder for some children to settle. Try adjusting the schedule to suit your child's age and observe how they respond.

### What if my baby suddenly starts waking more often after sleeping well?

Developmental changes, teething discomfort, changes in routine, or illness may affect sleep. If the change persists, your baby seems unwell, or you are concerned about their health, speak with a healthcare professional.
