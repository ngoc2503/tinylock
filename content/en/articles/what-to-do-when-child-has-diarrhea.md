Diarrhea is a common problem in young children. A child may have frequent loose or watery stools, sometimes accompanied by stomach pain, vomiting, or fever. Many cases improve within a few days, but parents need to pay particular attention to dehydration and electrolyte loss.

What should you do when your child has diarrhea? Should you stop giving food, use anti-diarrheal medicine, or simply encourage your child to drink more water?

This article explains how to care for a child with diarrhea at home, how to replace lost fluids safely, and which warning signs mean your child needs medical attention.

## 1. How can you tell if your child has diarrhea?

Diarrhea is generally defined as passing loose or watery stools three or more times within 24 hours, or having bowel movements that are clearly more frequent than usual for that child.

However, exclusively breastfed babies may naturally have soft, loose, and frequent stools. This can be normal if there is no significant change from their usual pattern.

Common signs of diarrhea include:

- More frequent bowel movements than usual.
- Loose, watery, or unformed stools.
- Mucus or an unusual smell in the stool.
- Stomach pain, fussiness, or discomfort.
- Nausea or vomiting.
- Fever, tiredness, or reduced appetite.

If you notice blood in the stool, severe abdominal pain, or signs of dehydration, seek medical advice promptly.

## 2. Why do children get diarrhea?

There are several possible causes of diarrhea in children. Gastrointestinal infections are among the most common.

### Viral infections

Viruses such as rotavirus and norovirus can cause gastroenteritis, leading to watery stools, vomiting, stomach pain, and sometimes fever.

Rotavirus is an important cause of severe diarrhea in young children. Rotavirus vaccination, according to the recommended schedule, helps reduce the risk of severe illness.

### Bacterial or parasitic infections

Children may become infected through contaminated food or water, or through poor hand hygiene.

Some infections can cause fever, significant abdominal pain, or bloody stools.

### Food and dietary factors

Some children may develop diarrhea after:
- Eating contaminated or improperly stored food.
- Making sudden changes to their diet.
- Consuming foods they cannot tolerate.
- Drinking milk or eating foods that are unsuitable for their age or needs.

### Medication side effects

Some medicines, particularly antibiotics, can change the balance of bacteria in the gut and cause diarrhea.

If diarrhea begins after your child starts a medication, ask a doctor or pharmacist for advice. Do not stop a prescribed medicine without professional guidance.

## 3. What should you do when your child has diarrhea?

The main goals are to replace lost water and electrolytes and maintain adequate nutrition so your child can recover.

### Step 1: Give oral rehydration solution (ORS)

Oral rehydration solution, commonly called ORS, replaces water and essential salts lost through diarrhea and vomiting.

Parents should:
- Choose an appropriate ORS product that has not expired.
- Mix the packet with exactly the amount of water stated on the package.
- Use safe, clean water.
- Never add extra sugar, salt, milk, or fruit juice.
- Do not divide a packet by guesswork unless the manufacturer provides instructions.
- Store and use the prepared solution according to the package directions.

**How to give ORS:**

- Offer small sips or spoonfuls frequently.
- If your child vomits, pause for about 5–10 minutes and then restart more slowly.
- If your child refuses to drink, keeps vomiting, or appears weaker, seek medical care.

Plain water alone may not adequately replace the electrolytes lost during significant diarrhea.

### Step 2: Continue breastfeeding or formula feeding

If your baby is breastfed, continue breastfeeding frequently and whenever your baby wants. Breast milk provides fluids, nutrition, and immune-protective factors.

If your child drinks infant formula, usually continue the normal formula prepared according to the manufacturer's instructions, unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.

Do not dilute formula or switch to a different type of milk without medical advice.

### Step 3: Do not make your child fast

Fasting is not a treatment for diarrhea. Children still need energy and nutrients to recover.

If your child has started eating solid foods:
- Offer smaller meals more frequently.
- Choose soft, easy-to-eat foods appropriate for their age.
- Maintain a varied, balanced diet.
- Do not force your child to eat when feeling nauseated, but continue offering small amounts.
- Return to the usual age-appropriate diet as soon as your child can tolerate it.

Suitable foods may include porridge, soft rice, potatoes, lean meat, eggs, and properly prepared vegetables.

There is no need to restrict children to plain rice porridge or follow a prolonged restrictive diet.

### Step 4: Monitor bowel movements and urination

Keep track of:
- How many times your child passes stool each day.
- Whether stools are loose or watery, and whether they contain blood or mucus.
- How often your child vomits.
- Whether your child can drink water and ORS.
- Whether urination is less frequent than usual.
- Whether your child is alert, active, and responding normally.

These details can help you recognize worsening symptoms and provide useful information if your child needs medical attention.

### Step 5: Maintain good hygiene to prevent infection

If diarrhea is caused by an infection, it can spread through contaminated hands, food, water, and surfaces.

Parents should:
- Wash hands with soap after changing diapers or using the toilet.
- Wash hands before preparing food and feeding children.
- Clean diaper-changing areas and toilets regularly.
- Use safe water when preparing food.
- Cook food thoroughly and store it properly.
- Avoid sharing towels, cups, or eating utensils that have not been cleaned.

## 4. What should children with diarrhea eat and avoid?

A suitable diet helps children maintain energy and recover.

| Recommended foods and drinks | Limit or avoid |
|---|---|
| Breast milk | Stopping breastfeeding without medical advice |
| Formula prepared correctly | Diluting formula |
| Porridge, soft rice, potatoes | Very fatty foods if they worsen discomfort |
| Properly cooked meat, fish, and eggs | Raw or undercooked foods |
| Soft-cooked vegetables and age-appropriate fruit | Soft drinks and carbonated beverages |
| ORS when fluid replacement is needed | Very sweet fruit juice and sugary drinks |

Some soft drinks, packaged fruit juices, and other sugary beverages may make diarrhea worse. Do not use sports drinks, soda, or fruit juice as substitutes for ORS.

## 5. Should you give your child anti-diarrheal medicine?

Parents should not give anti-diarrheal medicines to children without medical advice.

Some medicines that slow bowel movements may be unsafe for young children and do not treat the underlying cause. Antibiotics are also not routinely needed for every case of diarrhea because many cases are caused by viruses.

Important points:

- **Anti-diarrheal medicines:** Do not give them to children without professional advice, especially young children.
- **Antibiotics:** Use only when a doctor determines that they are appropriate.
- **Zinc:** A healthcare professional may recommend zinc for children with diarrhea, depending on the child's age and applicable local treatment guidelines. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about the appropriate dose and duration.
- **Probiotics:** Not all products have the same evidence of benefit. Probiotics should not replace ORS or medical assessment when warning signs are present.

Never combine several medicines or use a prescription intended for someone else.

## 6. Signs of dehydration parents should recognize

Dehydration is the most important complication to watch for when a child has diarrhea. Young children, especially babies under six months, can become dehydrated more quickly.

Possible signs include:

- Dry mouth and tongue.
- Crying with fewer tears or no tears.
- Eyes that look more sunken than usual.
- Less frequent urination or fewer wet diapers.
- Unusual thirst or repeatedly asking for drinks.
- Reduced skin elasticity.
- Tiredness, reduced activity, or unusual irritability.

**Signs of severe dehydration that require emergency care:**

- The child is very sleepy, difficult to wake, or poorly responsive.
- The child cannot drink or breastfeed.
- Cold hands and feet, pale skin, or mottled skin.
- Fast or abnormal breathing.
- Markedly sunken eyes or a rapidly worsening general condition.

If your child shows signs of severe dehydration, seek emergency medical care immediately. Do not delay getting help while trying to make your child finish a certain amount of ORS at home.

## 7. When should you take your child to a doctor?

Seek medical assessment promptly if your child has any of the following:

- Diarrhea in a baby under six months, especially if stools are frequent or feeding is reduced.
- Signs of dehydration.
- Blood in the stool or unusually black stools.
- High fever or fever accompanied by a worsening general condition.
- Severe abdominal pain or a swollen abdomen.
- Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down.
- Refusal to breastfeed, drink, or a marked reduction in urination.
- Diarrhea lasting more than seven days without improvement.
- An underlying medical condition, malnutrition, or a weakened immune system.

If your child is difficult to wake, cannot drink, has cold extremities, or shows signs of shock, seek emergency care immediately.

Do not wait until several warning signs appear before getting medical help.

## 8. How long does diarrhea usually last in children?

Many cases of acute infectious diarrhea improve within about 5–7 days. Some children may continue to have loose stools for longer, even after other symptoms have improved.

If your child is not getting better, has prolonged or recurrent diarrhea, or develops unusual symptoms, arrange a medical assessment to identify the cause.

Do not continue relying on home care indefinitely just because your child is still able to eat and drink.

## 9. How can you prevent diarrhea in children?

Prevention can reduce the risk of diarrhea and help stop infections from spreading within the family.

Parents can take these steps:

1. **Wash hands with soap:** Wash before preparing food, before feeding your child, and after changing diapers.
2. **Practice food safety:** Choose fresh food, cook it thoroughly, and store it properly.
3. **Use safe water:** Give children safe drinking water and clean feeding bottles and utensils properly.
4. **Breastfeed:** Encourage exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months when possible, then continue breastfeeding alongside age-appropriate complementary foods.
5. **Vaccinate against rotavirus:** Follow the vaccination schedule and recommendations of your healthcare provider.
6. **Keep the environment clean:** Regularly clean toilets, diaper-changing areas, and frequently used toys.

## 10. Conclusion

When your child has diarrhea, the most important steps are to replace lost fluids and electrolytes correctly, continue breastfeeding and age-appropriate feeding, and monitor closely for signs of dehydration.

Use ORS according to the package instructions. Do not give anti-diarrheal medicines or antibiotics without medical advice. If your child has bloody stools, persistent vomiting, refuses feeds, urinates much less than usual, becomes unusually sleepy, or shows signs of severe dehydration, seek medical care immediately.

Appropriate home care and early recognition of warning signs can help prevent complications and support a safer recovery.
