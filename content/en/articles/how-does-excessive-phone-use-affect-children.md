Smartphones are becoming increasingly common in everyday life. Many parents let their children watch videos, play games, or use apps for entertainment and learning. Sometimes, phones are also used to keep children occupied during meals or outings.

However, excessive phone use, especially when children spend long periods watching screens alone, may affect their sleep, eyesight, physical activity, language development, attention, and emotional well-being.

So, how does excessive phone use affect children? Does using a phone always harm a child? This article explains the possible effects and helps parents recognize when screen habits may be interfering with their child's daily life.

## 1. Excessive phone use can affect children's sleep

This is one of the most important concerns associated with unhealthy screen habits.

Screen light, stimulating videos, and using a phone close to bedtime can make it harder for children to relax, fall asleep, or get enough sleep.

Parents may notice that their child:

- Frequently asks to watch videos before bedtime.
- Has difficulty falling asleep or regularly goes to bed late.
- Wakes up frequently or struggles to get up in the morning.
- Feels tired or sleepy and has difficulty concentrating the next day.
- Becomes more irritable when sleep-deprived.

If this pattern continues, insufficient sleep may affect learning, mood, memory, and overall health.

**What can parents do?**

- Avoid screen use during the hour before bedtime.
- Keep phones and tablets out of children's sleeping areas.
- Maintain relatively consistent bedtimes and wake-up times.
- Replace evening screen time with reading, quiet music, or conversations with parents.

## 2. It may affect eyesight and eye health

Looking at a screen at close range for extended periods may cause eye strain and discomfort.

Common symptoms include:

- Tired or aching eyes.
- Dry eyes, frequent blinking, or eye rubbing.
- Headaches after prolonged screen use.
- Temporary blurred vision or difficulty refocusing when looking from near to far.

In addition, prolonged screen use and insufficient outdoor activity are associated with an increased risk of myopia and myopia progression in children.

However, it would be inaccurate to conclude that simply using a phone will definitely cause nearsightedness or permanent eye damage. Risk is also influenced by genetics, near-work habits, the learning environment, and time spent outdoors.

**What can parents do?**

- Encourage regular eye breaks during screen use.
- Maintain an appropriate viewing distance and discourage children from holding phones too close to their faces.
- Avoid letting children use phones in very dark rooms or while lying down.
- Encourage outdoor play and frequent distance viewing.
- Arrange an eye examination if your child frequently squints, holds objects unusually close, complains of blurred vision, or has recurring headaches.

## 3. It may affect language development and communication skills

Young children primarily learn to speak through listening, observing, imitating, and interacting directly with people around them.

When children spend too much time watching videos alone, they may miss opportunities to talk, ask questions, listen, and practice communication.

Possible effects include:

- Less spontaneous conversation with parents and other people.
- Fewer opportunities to express needs, thoughts, and feelings verbally.
- Reduced opportunities to learn new words through real-life interactions.
- Less practice with back-and-forth conversations and social communication.

Direct interaction with caregivers is particularly important for language development in children under three.

However, screen use is not the only possible cause of delayed speech. Language development is also influenced by hearing, communication opportunities, individual developmental differences, and many other factors.

**What can parents do?**

- Spend time talking directly with their children every day.
- Read books, tell stories, and ask questions that encourage responses.
- Encourage children to describe objects, events, and feelings.
- If watching educational content together, discuss and explain it rather than leaving children to watch alone.
- Arrange an assessment if your child shows signs of delayed speech or loses previously acquired communication skills.

## 4. It may reduce attention and affect learning

Some videos and games feature rapidly changing images, stimulating sounds, eye-catching effects, and instant rewards. Frequent exposure to this type of content may make children accustomed to fast-paced entertainment and less comfortable with activities that require patience.

Parents may notice that their child:

- Gets distracted easily while reading or doing homework.
- Quickly loses interest in activities that take time to complete.
- Frequently wants to switch videos or games.
- Has difficulty staying focused on one activity.
- Avoids less stimulating activities such as drawing, building puzzles, or reading.

Excessive screen use has been associated with weaker attention control and less favorable academic outcomes. However, phone use alone cannot establish that a child has ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder) or another developmental condition.

**What can parents do?**

- Break learning activities into manageable periods according to the child's age.
- Encourage reading, drawing, puzzles, and games that involve thinking.
- Limit content with excessively fast pacing or continuous autoplay.
- Keep phones away while children are studying or doing homework.
- Praise children for staying focused and completing activities.

## 5. It may increase sedentary behavior and affect body weight

When children spend long periods sitting and watching phones, they have fewer opportunities to run, walk, play sports, and enjoy outdoor activities.

Over time, this habit may lead to:

- Lower levels of physical activity and reduced physical endurance.
- More time spent sitting.
- An increased risk of unhealthy weight gain when calorie intake exceeds energy expenditure.
- Fewer opportunities to develop muscle strength, coordination, and balance.
- Missed opportunities to establish healthy exercise habits.

In addition, food advertisements and food-related content on video platforms may influence children's food preferences.

**What can parents do?**

- Encourage daily physical activity appropriate for the child's age.
- Provide opportunities for outdoor play, running, cycling, and sports.
- Avoid making phone use the main form of entertainment during free time.
- Avoid routinely allowing children to watch phones while eating.
- Help children choose enjoyable activities that do not require screens.

## 6. It may affect emotions and self-regulation

Some children become irritable, upset, or highly reactive when asked to stop using a phone.

This may be related to becoming accustomed to continuous entertainment, difficulty transitioning to other activities, or not yet having sufficient emotional self-regulation skills.

Parents may notice that their child:

- Cries, screams, or becomes angry when the phone is taken away.
- Repeatedly asks to use the phone despite being reminded of the limits.
- Struggles to play independently or find another activity without a device.
- Becomes impatient when asked to wait.
- Uses a phone to cope with sadness, anger, or frustration.

If parents frequently give children a phone whenever they cry or have a tantrum, children may have fewer opportunities to learn how to recognize and regulate their emotions.

This does not mean children will inevitably develop emotional or psychological problems. Emotional regulation is also influenced by age, temperament, family environment, and caregiver support.

**What can parents do?**

- Avoid using a phone as the only way to calm a crying child.
- Help children identify feelings such as sadness, anger, disappointment, and worry.
- Teach children to breathe, take a break, or move to an appropriate activity.
- Give children advance notice before screen time ends.
- Maintain consistent limits and respond calmly when children protest.

## 7. It may reduce family interaction and social skills

Children need direct social interaction to learn how to listen, share, take turns, cooperate, and understand other people's feelings.

If phones take up most of their free time, children may miss opportunities to develop these skills.

Possible signs include:

- Less conversation with parents and siblings.
- Less interest in group activities.
- Fewer opportunities to practice sharing toys and taking turns.
- Difficulty maintaining interactions during play with other children if they have limited practice.
- Preferring electronic devices over family activities.

Parents should also remember that frequently using phones during meals or conversations can reduce the quality of interactions with their children.

**What can parents do?**

- Spend time playing with children every day.
- Maintain phone-free family meals.
- Encourage group activities, physical games, and time with friends.
- Listen to children and invite them to talk about their day.
- Set a good example by limiting personal phone use when spending time with children.

## 8. It increases the risk of exposure to inappropriate content

The effects of phone use are not determined by screen time alone. The content children encounter is also important.

Without appropriate supervision, children may accidentally encounter:

- Violent videos, dangerous behavior, or unsafe challenges.
- Profanity or content inappropriate for their age.
- Advertisements for unsuitable foods, products, or games.
- Content that causes fear, anxiety, or intense emotional reactions.
- Games with in-app purchases, advertisements, or interactions with strangers.

For older children, unsupervised use of social media and online platforms may also expose them to cyberbullying, personal information sharing, and contact with people who have harmful intentions.

**What can parents do?**

- Choose apps and content appropriate for the child's age.
- Prioritize educational, creative, and thought-provoking content.
- Use parental controls to manage apps, screen time, and content.
- Talk with children about what they watch and experience online.
- Teach children not to share their addresses, phone numbers, private images, or personal information with strangers.
- Do not allow children unrestricted access to social media before they are ready to recognize online risks.

## 9. How much screen time should children have each day?

There is no single time limit that is appropriate for every child. Parents should consider age, purpose of use, content, sleep, physical activity, and whether screen use interferes with daily life.

The World Health Organization (WHO) provides recommendations for sedentary screen time among children under five:

| Age | Recommended screen time |
|---|---|
| Under 1 year | Sedentary screen time is not recommended |
| 1 year | Sedentary screen time is not recommended |
| 2 years | No more than 1 hour per day; less is better |
| 3–4 years | No more than 1 hour per day; less is better |
| 5–11 years | Establish limits appropriate to sleep, learning, physical activity, and family life |

Note: WHO's recommendations for children under five refer to sedentary screen-based activities, not a universal limit for every type of device use. For older children, parents should develop family rules that suit their needs rather than applying one rigid number to everyone.

The most important principle is that phones should not take time away from sleep, learning, physical activity, reading, play, and direct social interaction.

## 10. When should parents pay special attention to their child's phone habits?

Parents should reassess their child's screen habits if they notice one or more of the following:

- Regularly staying up late or losing sleep because of phone use.
- Refusing outdoor activities or play with other people.
- Reacting intensely whenever screen time is limited.
- Being unable to play or relax independently without a phone.
- A noticeable decline in school performance or concentration.
- Frequent eye strain, blurred vision, or headaches.
- Less communication, reduced conversation, or significant changes in mood and behavior.

If these signs persist and interfere with daily life, parents should consult a pediatrician or an appropriate specialist. If a child shows delayed language development, loses previously acquired skills, or experiences significant behavioral changes, do not assume that phone use is the only cause. A proper assessment is important.

## Conclusion

Excessive phone use may negatively affect children's sleep, eyesight, physical activity, language development, attention, emotions, and social skills. However, the effects are not the same for every child. They also depend on content, age, usage patterns, and parental involvement.

The goal is not necessarily to ban technology completely, but to help children develop balanced, limited, and age-appropriate digital habits.

Prioritize activities that support healthy development, including adequate sleep, physical activity, reading, creative play, and direct conversations with family members.
