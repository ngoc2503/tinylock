Many parents experience a common situation: their newborn sleeps peacefully in their arms but wakes up, startles, and cries as soon as they are placed in a crib or on a mattress. When picked up again, the baby may quickly settle down or fall asleep.

This can leave parents exhausted and worried, wondering whether they should continue holding their baby or teach them to sleep independently.

In fact, this behavior is quite common during the first months of life. Newborns are still adjusting to the world outside the womb and often need closeness, warmth, and caregiver support to feel calm. However, parents also need to learn how to transfer their baby safely and provide a safe sleep environment.

## 1. Why does a newborn cry whenever they are put down?

There are several reasons why a baby may become uncomfortable or wake up when moved out of an adult's arms.

### Your baby needs closeness and physical comfort

Inside the womb, babies are surrounded by warmth, movement, and familiar sounds. After birth, lying alone on a large, flat surface with little physical contact may feel unfamiliar.

When held, babies can sense their caregiver's warmth, familiar smell, breathing, and heartbeat. These sensations may help them feel calmer and more secure.

### Your baby may not be in deep sleep yet

Some babies wake up when they are placed down because they are still in light sleep or transitioning between sleep cycles. Changes in position, temperature, or physical contact can wake them.

### The Moro reflex (startle reflex)

Newborns have a natural startle reflex. When they sense a sudden change in position or a loss of support, they may suddenly extend their arms and legs and begin crying.

This reflex is common in the first months of life and gradually decreases as the nervous system develops.

### Your baby may be hungry or not fully fed

A newborn's stomach is small, so babies need frequent feeds throughout the day and night. If your baby has not had enough milk or is approaching the next feeding, they may become unsettled and struggle to fall asleep again.

### Gas or discomfort after feeding

Some babies swallow air while feeding, which can cause discomfort, burping, or fussiness. If your baby often cries after feeds, spits up frequently, or seems uncomfortable when lying down, monitor the symptoms and discuss them with a healthcare professional.

### Your baby may be overtired or overstimulated

Bright lights, loud noises, frequent handling by different people, or staying awake for too long may make it difficult for a baby to relax. When overtired, some babies actually find it harder to fall asleep and may become more unsettled.

## 2. What should parents do when their newborn cries every time they are put down?

You do not need to force your baby to sleep alone immediately. First, check their basic needs, help them settle, and then try transferring them gently.

### Step 1: Check your baby's basic needs

Before putting your baby to sleep, check:

- Is your baby hungry or due for a feed?
- Is the diaper wet, dirty, or uncomfortable?
- Are the clothes too tight, too warm, or too cold?
- Is a strand of hair or thread wrapped around a finger or toe?
- Does your baby show signs of gas, discomfort, or illness?

If your baby shows signs of hunger, feed them according to their needs and age-appropriate feeding guidance.

### Step 2: Help your baby calm down before putting them down

You can try:

- Holding your baby close to your chest and practicing skin-to-skin contact while you are fully awake and able to supervise.
- Gently swaying your baby in your arms.
- Speaking softly or singing in a steady, soothing voice.
- Gently patting your baby's back or bottom while holding them.
- Reducing bright lights and noise in the room.

Never shake, toss, or jolt your baby to make them fall asleep.

### Step 3: Try putting your baby down once they are relaxed

When your baby has settled and shows signs of sleepiness, you can try the following:

1. Keep your baby close to your body for a little longer so they can settle.
2. Slowly lower your baby into a crib or bassinet with a firm, flat sleep surface.
3. Gently place the lower body down first, followed by the shoulders and head.
4. If your baby remains calm, keep your hand gently on their chest or tummy for a few seconds, then slowly remove it.
5. If your baby cries, check their needs and comfort them. You can try again once they have settled.

These steps are simply ways to help your baby transition from being held to lying on a sleep surface. Not every baby will adjust immediately. You may need to try several times and adapt to your baby's responses.

### Step 4: Create a calm and comfortable sleep environment

- Keep the room well ventilated and at a comfortable temperature.
- Dim the lights and reduce sudden noises.
- Change your baby's diaper and dress them appropriately before sleep.
- You can follow a simple routine such as feeding, burping if needed, changing the diaper, gentle cuddling, and then placing your baby down to sleep.

Newborns do not need a rigid sleep schedule. Observe their sleep cues and respond to their needs rather than forcing them to follow fixed times.

### Step 5: Respond to crying instead of assuming your baby is being difficult

During the first months of life, babies cannot deliberately manipulate their parents or regulate their emotions independently. Crying is their way of communicating their needs and seeking help.

Holding, comforting, and responding to your baby does not mean you are spoiling them. If your newborn needs to be held to settle, you can respond to that need while gradually helping them become familiar with a safe sleep space.

## 3. How can you gradually help your baby get used to sleeping in a crib?

Parents can introduce the crib gradually without rushing the process.

- **Start with an easier sleep period:** Choose a time when your baby has been fed, has a clean diaper, and is not overtired.
- **Create familiar sleep cues:** Keep pre-sleep activities gentle and consistent, such as dimming the lights, speaking softly, and cuddling.
- **Try putting your baby down when sleepy:** When your baby is relaxed and showing signs of sleepiness, try placing them in the crib. If they become very upset, check their needs and comfort them rather than forcing the process.
- **Gradually increase comfortable time in the sleep space:** You can begin with short periods when your baby is awake and supervised. Never leave your baby alone on an adult bed.
- **Be patient and flexible:** Every baby has a different rhythm. Some days your baby may settle easily; on other days, they may need more cuddling.

During the newborn stage, do not use prolonged unattended crying as a method of forcing your baby to fall asleep independently. Your baby needs a response when hungry, uncomfortable, or in need of comfort.

## 4. Important safe sleep rules every parent should know

Even if your baby sleeps better in your arms, safety must remain the priority whenever they sleep. According to the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP):

- **Always place your baby on their back for sleep**, both during naps and at night.
- Use a crib, bassinet, or sleep space designed for infants, with a firm, flat, noninclined mattress.
- Use only a fitted sheet. Keep pillows, loose blankets, stuffed toys, sleep positioners, and other soft objects out of the sleep area.
- Share a room with your baby, but use a separate sleep surface. Room-sharing is recommended, ideally for at least the first six months.
- Do not allow your baby to routinely sleep in a car seat, swing, stroller, reclined seat, or other sitting device. If your baby falls asleep in one of these devices, move them to a safe sleep surface on their back as soon as it is safe to do so.
- Never fall asleep with your baby on a sofa or armchair. These surfaces carry a high risk of trapping or suffocating an infant.
- Do not use pillows, rolled towels, or other objects to elevate your baby's head, even if they spit up frequently. Babies should still sleep on their backs on a firm, flat, noninclined surface unless a healthcare professional gives specific medical instructions.

**A note about swaddling:** Swaddling may help some babies startle less, but it must be done correctly. It should not be too tight around the chest or hips, and weighted swaddles should not be used. Stop swaddling as soon as your baby shows signs of trying to roll or begins rolling. A swaddled baby must still be placed on their back on a safe sleep surface.

## 5. When is crying a sign that your baby needs medical attention?

Do not assume that every baby who cries when put down simply wants to be held. Sometimes crying is a sign of illness or discomfort that needs medical assessment.

Contact a healthcare professional if your baby has any of the following:

- Unusually frequent crying, difficulty being comforted, or a cry that sounds different from normal.
- Poor feeding, refusing feeds, or drinking significantly less milk.
- Frequent or repeated vomiting, or projectile vomiting.
- A swollen abdomen, unusual bowel movements, or blood in the stool.
- Poor weight gain or a noticeable reduction in wet diapers.
- Prolonged crying, especially when accompanied by other unusual symptoms.

**Seek emergency medical care immediately** if your baby has any of these signs:

- A temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher in a baby younger than 3 months.
- Difficulty breathing, grunting, pauses in breathing, or blue lips or tongue.
- Seizures, unusual drowsiness, limpness, or difficulty waking.
- Green vomit, blood in vomit, or repeated projectile vomiting.
- A rash that does not fade when pressed.
- Inability to feed, signs of severe dehydration, or rapidly worsening condition.

If you feel that something is not right, seek medical assessment rather than focusing only on getting your baby to sleep.

## 6. What should parents do when they feel exhausted by their baby's crying?

A baby who repeatedly cries when put down can leave parents sleep-deprived, stressed, and exhausted. This is something many families experience, especially during the first weeks after birth.

Parents can:

- Take turns caring for the baby with a partner or family member when possible.
- Rest whenever another trusted caregiver is available to help.
- Ask family members to help with household tasks to reduce pressure.
- If you feel overwhelmed, place your baby on their back in a safe crib or bassinet with no soft objects, then step away for a few minutes to calm down.
- Ask someone you trust to take over if you feel unable to continue caring for your baby safely.

**Never shake, hit, or shout at your baby.** Shaking can cause serious brain injury, lifelong disability, or death, even if it happens for only a short time.

## 7. Frequently asked questions

**Is it normal for a newborn to sleep only when held?**

This is quite common during the first weeks of life. Your baby may need warmth, physical contact, and a sense of security from their caregiver. However, when you need to sleep or cannot safely continue holding your baby, place them on their back in a suitable crib or bassinet.

**Should I let my baby cry for a while before picking them up?**

During the newborn stage, respond to crying by checking your baby's needs and helping them feel secure. Do not deliberately leave your baby crying for prolonged periods to force independent sleep. If you feel overwhelmed, place your baby in a safe sleep space and step away briefly to calm down.

**My baby startles as soon as I put them down. Could this be the Moro reflex?**

Possibly. The Moro reflex is a natural newborn reflex that may occur when a baby senses a sudden change in position or hears an unexpected sound. It usually decreases as the baby develops. If your baby has unusual repeated movements accompanied by abnormal eye movements or changes in awareness, seek medical assessment.

**Should I place my baby on their side or use a pillow to help them sleep better?**

No. The recommended safe sleep position is on the back, on a firm, flat, noninclined surface. Do not use pillows, rolled towels, or other objects to support the head or position the baby.

**Will holding my baby too often make them dependent on being held?**

Holding your newborn and responding to their needs will not spoil them. During the first months, babies need closeness and care to feel secure. You can gradually help your baby become familiar with their own crib without stopping cuddles and comfort.

## Conclusion

A newborn who cries whenever they are put down may need closeness, may be experiencing the startle reflex, may not yet have stable sleep patterns, or may be uncomfortable because of hunger or gas. Parents should check their baby's needs, offer gentle comfort, and patiently help them become familiar with their own sleep space.

Most importantly, always place your baby on their back on a firm, flat, noninclined sleep surface with no soft objects. If your baby cries unusually, feeds poorly, develops a fever, or shows warning signs, seek medical care promptly.
