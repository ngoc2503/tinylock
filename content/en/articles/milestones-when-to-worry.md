Every child develops at their own pace, but most children reach basic skills in a fairly similar order. These skills are called **developmental milestones**, and they cover how a child plays, learns, speaks, behaves and moves.

## What milestones are

Knowing the milestones helps you see which behaviors fit your child's age. For example, a two-year-old who says "no" all the time is usually typical, while a two-year-old who never looks at you may be worth mentioning to a doctor.

## How to keep track

1. Use an age-based milestone checklist. The CDC (United States) offers free ones, plus the Milestone Tracker app.
2. Fill in the checklist at ages 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24 and 30 months, and at 3, 4 and 5 years.
3. Write down any questions to bring up at routine checkups.
4. Bring the completed checklist to the appointment.

## When to talk to a doctor

- Your child has not reached one or more milestones for their age
- Your child loses a skill they had before (speech, eye contact, play...)
- You feel something is not right, even if it is hard to put into words

Finding concerns early means support can start early, and early support often leads to better outcomes. Do not wait if you are worried.

## Please note

Milestone checklists help parents keep track but do not replace developmental screening with standardized tools by a health professional. The CDC checklists were developed for children in the United States, so also check with a pediatrician where you live.
